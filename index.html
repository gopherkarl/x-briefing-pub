"I 'rely' on explanations (e.g. that the plane will fly) only to the extent that they have no good rivals. The only alternative is to 'rely' on a bad explanation (e.g. that the terminal will fly) that does have a good rival. It is not because the winner has a reliability property..."

🔑 Key Takeaway

Relying on an explanation is not a property of the explanation itself but of its position in an ongoing competition against rivals. We are never choosing between reliability and nothing — we are always choosing the least bad explanation available. Justification is comparative and dynamical, decided by which conjecture has best survived criticism, not by any absolute stamp of certainty.
