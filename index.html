"There is no such thing as "evidence against" something in the absence of viable explanations contradicting it. At most, evidence could then make a theory problematic. A theory should be preferred when it explains what its rivals leave as a heap of anomalies they can't explain."

🔑 Key Takeaway

Scientific progress is not about accumulating 'evidence against' or 'for' theories in isolation. Rather, it is a competition between explanations. A theory remains the best explanation of reality until a rival theory is proposed that solves its problems and explains its anomalies.
