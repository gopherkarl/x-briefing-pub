"You become money by first becoming something people refuse to part with."

🔑 Key Takeaway

A conjectural account of how a monetary standard is established: not by decree, nor by intrinsic worth, but by the emergent property of becoming something people are reluctant to exchange away. This is a Popperian point about institutions: they are created and sustained by human expectation and conjecture, not by authority.

A Popperian corrective to a recurring error in political economy: treating trade as a zero-sum contest of national advantage is mercantilism, an explanation of wealth that has been refuted repeatedly yet persists on every side of politics. Getting this distinction right matters because bad economic explanations, once lodged, reliably produce policies that destroy wealth.
