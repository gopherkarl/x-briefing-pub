"A reason “maximise well being” accounts of morality fail is tied to the unpredictability of knowledge creation. Would devoting your life to seeking a cancer cure “maximise well being” or would working on quantum computing? We cannot possibly know the outcome of doing either."

🔑 Key Takeaway

The refutation of consequentialist maxim-think turns on the open-endedness of knowledge creation. If the right action is whichever maximises wellbeing over the whole future, then deciding requires knowing the downstream consequences of alternative life projects - but those consequences are exactly what nobody can foresee, because they depend on discoveries that will be made by other people with other projects in the interim. A cancer cure and a quantum computer are incommensurable bets precisely because each, if successful, changes the value of the other. So 'maximise wellbeing' offers no decision procedure for the very cases that matter most. The moral relevance is real but ineliminably speculative: it tells you to acquire more knowledge, not how to optimise against a future you cannot read.

"@Cato_The_Welder @jon3k Yes. That well captures what a “bad explanation” is in the sense of @DavidDeutschOxf . Such mysterious thumb drives could explain literally any hitherto unknown code (the truth is, of course, *people* are fundamentally behind such technology - always)."

🔑 Key Takeaway

A reminder of what makes an explanation bad in the Popperian-Deutschian sense: not that it is false, but that it could account for any state of affairs. A thumb drive that can explain any unknown code is a swiss-army explanation - it adapts to whatever needs explaining and thereby explains nothing, because it rules out no possibility and is consistent with every alternative. The corrective is that the technology was built by people with purposes, errors, and revisions, so the good explanation points at those agents and their deliberate choices rather than at a mystical artefact. An explanation earns its place not by being able to fit everything but by being hard to vary while still working - and the account that survives that test here is ordinary human engineering.

"Any fad has the potential to strain relationships between participants and non-participants. But the current batch of Woke-based fads have as their central feature the replacing of friend and family relationships with spite and hatred. So they have all merged with the Pattern."

🔑 Key Takeaway

Distinguishes the ordinary harm of a fad from a distinctly moral harm. Any enthusiasm can mildly strain relations between the eager and the indifferent. The destructive version does something stronger: it redefines other people as targets rather than companions, so that the energy that once picked out shared aims now feeds on hostility to neighbours. Naming this as 'the Pattern' connects it to a recognisable diagnosis that recurs across ideologies - the recruitment of personal loyalty into institutionalised enmity. On this framing, what matters is not the particular slogans but the standing invitation to see those closest as enemies, which is a testable claim about consequences rather than a matter of taste.

David Deutsch @DavidDeutschOxf
🗓️ Published on September 20, 2026

"Knowledge, bah! Who needs more knowledge? It is well known for having Side-Effects. Whereas ignorance never hurt anyone! https://t.co/cdOg8WKC09"

🔑 Key Takeaway

A satire of the anti-knowledge posture that treats every harm as a reason to avoid improvement. The joke does the argumentative work by inverting the truthful claim: without exception, the catastrophes of human history have been produced by insufficient knowledge, not by too much of it. A technology developed with inadequate understanding has side-effects that are precisely the costs of ignorance; the remedy to a bad consequence is never less knowledge but more. The sarcasm makes a real distinction visible - between knowledge and the defective uses of knowing - and lines up against the recurring fallacy that the way to avoid the misuse of a capability is to remain ignorant of it. The cost of that path is the acceptance of every fixable evil as permanent.

The denial of the trade-off as the economic core of the argument. The claim put into the mouths of politicians and pollsters is that inflation and unemployment are a menu you can pick from - buy more growth now, pay the price later. Against that, the reply is that they are not alternatives at all: debasing the currency does not create real employment, it merely prices the same work in cheaper units, and the future hardship is not a separate 'option' but the inevitable consequence of the same policy. Refusing to reflate is, on this view, refusing a false choice that would forgive the present at the expense of the future. The fiscal discipline is presented as a commitment to the value of money and therefore to everyone whose savings are denominated in it.

"ZEC may be the only asset that appears cheaper as its price goes up."

🔑 Key Takeaway

A deflationary observation that turns the usual price narrative on its head. For most assets a rising market price makes them feel dearer, so holders and potential buyers read it as expensive. If something tends to appear more undervalued as its price climbs, what is being priced is not scarcity of supply tokens but the growing gap between the current price and the value of the underlying knowledge - a formally verified, privacy-preserving, self-sovereign money. The market, on this reading, is slowly catching up to an improvement that was already real when it was much cheaper, which is what makes each higher print look even more of a bargain. Returns come from betting on the creation of genuine new knowledge rather than on re-rating of a fixed thing.

🗓️ Published on September 18, 2026

"People who want to appear moral are attracted to the left. People who want to appear rational are attracted to the right. Effective Altruism lets you appear rational and moral - the ultimate ego trip."

🔑 Key Takeaway

An explanation of ideological attraction in terms of the status each side seems to confer rather than the truth of its doctrine. Signalling account rather than substance account: one tribe rewards the display of compassion, the other rewards the display of clear-headedness, and a movement that has learned to display both grants the strongest signal of all - the feeling of being at once the kindest and the smartest person in the room. The sting is that this is a claim about the people making the judgement, not about the merits of the positions. It is testable in the same way as any motivation-scepticism: does the attraction track the evidence, or does it track how the adoption makes the adopter appear? Pointing at the appearance is how the argument avoids being dismissed as mere debunking - it names a specific, in-principle separable mechanism.
