David Deutsch @DavidDeutschOxf
🗓️ Published on October 4, 2026

"@Dr_AamirIslam Nothing "ultimately distinguishes" anything from anything. All our ideas are conjectural. Denying that they are conjectural means refusing to try to correct errors in them."

🔑 Key Takeaway

A compact statement of fallibilism as the engine of error-correction. The phrase 'ultimately distinguishes' presumes a bedrock, a final ground of certainty that nothing actually has - because every idea, including the very criteria of distinction, is conjectural. Deutsch's move is to show that calling an idea conjectural is not an insult but the precondition of its improvement: an idea you treat as settled is an idea you refuse to test, and therefore an idea whose errors you refuse to find. The claim is thus the foundation of the whole feed's method - progress happens only where ideas are held as provisional hypotheses open to refutation.

A counterintuitive observation about the culture of ideas: the people most immersed in processing ideas are often the most susceptible to intellectual fads. Deutsch's point is that familiarity with many ideas, in itself, is not the same as possessing the critical-rationalist disposition to test and discard bad ones. An overactive idea-processing community can reward the novel, the fashionable, and the internally consistent over the true, so sophistication in handling arguments can coexist with - even feed - the acceptance of fashionable error. The remedy is the explicit norm of seeking refutations, not merely adding to the volume of processed ideas.
