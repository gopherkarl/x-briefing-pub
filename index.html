🗓️ Published on August 16, 2026

"Heisenberg invented matrix mechanics without knowing what matrices are. They weren't in any of his courses, because no one knew they were foundations. Born had to tell him afterwards that they were called matrix algebra."

🔑 Key Takeaway

Breakthrough knowledge does not require knowing the existing vocabulary of its own domain — in fact it usually cannot. Heisenberg created a new theory while ignorant of the formal name for its mathematics, showing that progress comes from seeking good explanations, not from mastering the established categories that might constrain them.

"Isn't the moral significance of plagiarism different in fields where there is no such thing as progress? Can you steal credit for someone's achievement if new achievements are forbidden? Yes, stealing counterfeit money is theft, but it's not morally equivalent to bank robbery."

🔑 Key Takeaway

The value of an achievement depends on whether it opens the door to future achievement. In a field where progress is forbidden, credit is a zero-sum commodity; where explanation is unbounded, each contribution compounds into more. This is why the moral weight of stealing credit tracks whether the stolen work was a step in an ongoing, world-changing conversation.
